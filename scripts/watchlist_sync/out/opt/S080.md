Stress (skew ratio ±25 % on calls and on put wings): base -5.3% · rich -5.3% · cheap -5.3%. The stress barely moves a **per $ debit** return by construction — a multiplicative skew ratio scales the entry debit and the exit value together, so it cancels except where the moving |Δ| crosses a ratio cell or a barrier fires a day earlier. The pricing risk this study carries is therefore in the *level of the barriers being hit*, not in the mean.

### The barrier rule vs holding to expiry (same contracts, same entries)

Mean return per $ debit, **gross of the T-bill subtraction** (so the barrier row differs in the last decimal from the arms table above, which is net).

| exit rule | all | long calls | long puts | merged (e) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| barriers (+100 / −50 / 21 DTE) | -8.3% | -5.8% | -10.7% | -5.2% |
| held to expiry, intrinsic | +1.5% | +17.8% | -14.8% | -29.9% |

Holding the same 0.50Δ ~45 DTE calls to expiry gives +17.8% per $ debit against -5.8% with the barriers — the pre-registered exit rule is itself a large negative. It caps the right tail at +100 % (where long calls earn everything) while taking the full downside, and it stops out 55% of trades. This is the mechanical reason S076's +14 % long call (Monday entries, 0.40Δ, held to expiry, IV/RV ≤ 0.95 slice) does not reappear here, and it is the first thing to change before any product.

## Known weaknesses

- Call skew still rests on one day of Schwab ladders (S075); the ±25 % stress is the guard, not a fix.
- Continuous strikes at the flat-IV Δ; real chains round to listed strikes.
- Barrier exits use daily closes: the +100 % take is recorded as exactly +100 % (limit), the −50 % stop takes the close in full (gap risk counted). No intraday path.
- Daily entries overlap heavily (45 DTE held up to 24 days) → month-block bootstrap, not iid t.
- lab_tech is a Monday panel forward-filled inside the week.
- Real option closes only exist from 2024-10 (Polygon plan limit, probed 10-22); everything earlier is model-priced.
