## 3. Hypothesis and analysis

**Jamie's hypothesis:** in a sentiment-led complex selloff, the market sells the *theme*, not the balance sheet. Peers with survivable sheets (low leverage, real coverage, liquidity) that fell *with* the bellwether mean-revert faster once the trigger name stabilises. CRWV itself is the wrong vehicle — its financing risk is real even though its demand is real — but quality peers carry the same demand beta with balance sheets that survive the wait.

**1st-level analysis (Muse):**
- The Oct 2026 drawdown was indiscriminate on price: CRWV −28.8% 1M, but also NBIS −27.3%, IREN −42.7%, APLD −26.5% — despite NBIS being profitable with a 4.03 current ratio and IREN holding $5.9B cash against D/E 1.8. Price contagion ≠ fundamental contagion; that gap is the trade.
- The trigger's risk is idiosyncratic (a refinancing/coverage question), while the peers' drawdowns are mostly beta to the theme. Idiosyncratic triggers with thematic contagion is the classic setup where quality-sorting should work.
- Asymmetry for the book: put premium on the whole complex is elevated post-selloff. Selling it on the bellwether concentrates exactly the tail that caused the selloff; selling it on quality peers harvests the same fear premium against a survivable balance sheet.

**2nd-level analysis (Muse):**
- **Stabilisation condition matters.** Quality peers likely don't mean-revert while the trigger is still making new lows (contagion continues). The study should test whether the effect is conditional on the bellwether's trough (e.g., no new low for 3 sessions) rather than on the calendar.
- **Survivorship of the theme.** If the trigger's problem turns out to be sector-real (AI capex financing dries up for everyone), quality peers are just slower losers. The controls (WULF/CORZ) exist to detect this: if the worst sheets fall *least* or recover *most*, the thesis is wrong, not early.
- **Earnings inside the window.** CORZ reports Oct 23, IREN ~Nov 5, CRWV ~Nov 9–10. Prints inside the forward window are both catalyst and confound — handle explicitly in the design (§4d), don't pretend they're noise.
- **Float/short-interest mechanics.** WULF at 24% short interest can squeeze violently and poison a naive relative-return read; quality sorting must be on balance-sheet families, not on "fell the most."

## 4. Study design

**(a) Quality screen.** Apply fildi's fund-score families — leverage, coverage, liquidity, profitability — to the neocloud / AI-infra peer set: CRWV, NBIS, IREN, APLD, WULF, CORZ, plus any fildi-universe names Claude adds (§7). Output: a quality rank on golden-source data with pinned definitions (Step 1). The screen must use the same family machinery as production scoring so the result doubles as a score validation (§6 iii).

**(b) Event study (historical).** Episodes = bellwether-led complex drawdowns: the live Oct 2026 instance; CRWV-led drawdowns since its Mar 2025 IPO; earlier AI-infra selloffs on the peers' longer histories (several peers predate CRWV's listing). Outcome: forward relative return vs the complex median at 5/10/20 sessions post-trough, sorted by quality rank from (a). **Small-n honesty:** the neocloud complex is young; n will be small and overlapping episodes are not independent. Report n and the overlap treatment explicitly — a suggestive result on n=6 is worth less than a clean null, and the forward test (d) is the real arbiter.

**(c) Options leg — the book's actual question.** Put-sale performance on quality peers post-selloff vs on the bellwether: IV rank at entry, realised vol over the holding window, assignment frequency, and worst-case drawdown of the short. Comparable-IV-rank matching so the comparison is about the underlier, not the entry. Historical leg uses whatever chain history fildi legitimately has (LSEG optlab + Schwab-forward accumulation per A228 — sparse; state coverage limits rather than backfilling from a non-golden source).

**(d) FORWARD TEST — pre-registered tonight, this is the falsifiable core.** From the Oct 21, 2026 close: **NBIS + IREN are the primary candidates, APLD secondary** (already +8.5% on Oct 21 — partially inflected, so it is tracked but not counted as a clean entry). Controls: CRWV (the trigger) and WULF/CORZ (worse-sheet falsification set). Track 20 sessions: relative return vs the complex median, max drawdown, and a paper put-sale on each candidate vs CRWV at matched IV rank. Earnings prints inside the window (CORZ Oct 23, IREN ~Nov 5, CRWV ~Nov 9–10) are logged as events, and results reported both including and excluding print-day moves. Pre-registration is the point: tonight's call gets graded, not narrated.

## 5. Pass / fail bars (Muse's draft — Claude finalises per §7)

- **Event study:** rank correlation between quality rank (a) and forward relative return at 10–20 sessions is positive and material across episodes; report the coefficient and n, no p-value theatre on tiny samples.
- **Forward test:** NBIS + IREN beat the complex median by **≥5 pts at 20 sessions** with **no worse max drawdown than CRWV**. Either leg failing = the test fails.
- **Options leg:** quality-peer put sales show a **lower assignment rate than bellwether put sales at comparable IV rank**, without giving back the edge in premium captured.
- **Fail is a publishable result.** If contagion is indiscriminate or quality doesn't sort recovery, the study says so — that would mean fildi's leverage/coverage families have no timing value in theme selloffs, which is itself decision-grade information for the book. No silent burial; the verdict lands in this file either way.

## 6. Use cases

1. **Standing news-base playbook output.** This study is instance #1 of a repeatable motion: bellwether takes a press-driven drawdown → Muse's scan hands Claude a ranked candidate list within a day, with the trigger analysis and the golden-source gate already done. *Why:* Jamie is the connection-maker's principal, not the screener — the playbook converts a headline into a testable relative-value question while the dislocation is still priced. *Expected value:* every future theme selloff (there will be more — AI infra, and beyond) starts from a ranked list and a pre-registered test instead of from opinions.
2. **Book application — migrate post-selloff put entries to quality peers.** If the study passes, post-selloff premium selling shifts from the trigger name to the quality peers: same fear premium, survivable tail, lower assignment risk. *Why:* the book's edge is selling overpriced fear; this aims it at names where assignment is an inconvenience, not a margin event. *Expected value:* fewer blow-up assignments per unit of premium in exactly the windows where premium is richest.
3. **Score validation.** The study is a live out-of-sample check on whether fildi's leverage/coverage/liquidity families predict *recovery ordering* — a use the fund score was not explicitly built for. *Why:* if the families sort recoveries, they earn weight in book-risk thinking (cf. A221 concentration/covariance); if they don't, that's a calibration fact Claude should know before the next study leans on them. *Expected value:* the score gets more trustworthy either way — confirmed or corrected.
