## Findings (claim / evidence / suggested fix)

**1. Tech: the `ma50Slope10d` part is a constant (High).**
- Evidence: `technicals-math.ts:217` stores slope as a fraction rounded to 2 dp (`r2`). Live `ticker_technicals` p1/p25/p50/p75/p99 = −0.11/−0.03/0/0.01/0.07; 143 of 955 are exactly 0. `c01(0.5 + slope/4)` → 0.47–0.52 for 98 % of names. So trendContext = mean(regime, MA200, ≈0.5): the slope adds nothing and pulls the part toward neutral by a third. The tech-1.1.0 IC gain came from regime + MA200 + zeroing volatilityState/volumeConfirm, not from slope.
- Suggested fix, in order:
  - (a) Producer: store 4 dp, not 2. A 1 % step over 10 days is coarse for a 50-day MA.
  - (b) Normalise by the stock's own vol so names compare: `slopeZ = ma50Slope10d / (atrPct × √10)`. That makes it a drift t-stat: a 3 % rise is noise for a 4 %/day name and a strong trend for a 1 %/day name.
  - (c) Use it the way the literature does (below), not as a linear average.

  **What MA50 slope is for.** It measures trend direction. Brock-Lakonishok-LeBaron 1992 and Faber 2007 use moving-average rules as trend filters, and Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen 2012 document time-series momentum. Three uses fit puts:
  1. **Regime gate:** rising MA50 (slopeZ > +0.5) = uptrend; flat = range; falling = downtrend.
  2. **Pullback in uptrend (interaction, not average):** short-term reversal (Jegadeesh 1990, Lehmann 1990) works best inside intermediate momentum (Jegadeesh-Titman 1993). A dip toward or below a *rising* MA50 is the classic entry, and Jamie's put-entry setup.
     - The old trendContext tried this but *averaged* "bullish" with "below MA50", so they cancelled. Your fix dropped the dip side instead of combining it.
     - Proposed form: `trendContext = uptrendGate(slopeZ, priceVsMa200Atr) × (0.5 + 0.5·dipDepth(priceVsMa50Atr))`, with the gate at ~0.3 in a downtrend, so a falling-knife dip scores low.
  3. **Projected support for strike buffer:** `MA50_at_expiry ≈ MA50 × (1 + slope × DTE/10)`. A strike under the projected rising MA50 has a support cushion, so add "strike vs projected MA50 in ATR" as a buffer input to the options score. Also flag a slope that turns from + to − after a run as trend exhaustion, a warning for new short puts.
- **Pre-registered test (study contract 12/14):**
  - Cells = slopeZ {up > +0.5, flat, down < −0.5} × priceVsMa50Atr {above 0, dip ≤ −1}.
  - Expected sign for put_kept and put P&L incl. premium: up+dip **+** (best: richer premium plus trend support) · up+above **+** (kept, thinner premium) · flat **0** · down+dip **−** (falling knife) · down+above **0/−**.
  - ≥ 30 trades per cell, week-clustered SEs, first-half fit / second-half test.
  - Ship only if the interaction beats tech-1.1.0's trendContext out of sample on put P&L, not just put_kept IC. put_kept rewards momentum that collects less premium.

**5. Outliers: diagnose each one, don't null it per ticker (High, applies to merge-trusted + fund + FV).**
- The scorer already handles real extremes. `normalizeCohort` winsorises at 2/98 % and then z-scores, so a genuine outlier gets the top score without skewing the cohort. Nulling a cell instead makes the family average the other metrics (they gain weight for that ticker only) and shrinks the family toward 50 by √coverage (`shrinkToNeutral`).
- So per-ticker nulls bias exactly the extreme names, which are the opportunities we look for, toward average.
- Example from the `roic` > 500 % clear (sources today): FTNT −713 %, MANH −1,555 %, MEDP −1,172 %, MCK +1,398 %, CPNG −584 %. Most are high-return businesses whose invested capital is small or negative after buybacks. The sign flips; the business is not bad. Nulling hides their quality; leaving −1,555 % would rank them worst. Neither is right.
- Suggested rule (one home, `FACTOR_MAP` + `recurring-bugs.md`). Classify every extreme before acting:
  1. **Data error** (wrong share class, sub-line revenue, unit/currency) → repair from the right source or convert. BRK.B TBVPS = Class A ÷ 1,500 can be derived; don't null it. Null only when nothing correct exists, and log a `data_issues` row (the ROIC clear logs nothing today).
  2. **Metric not meaningful for the business model** (ROIC/P/B for banks and brokers, P/B for negative-equity buyback names) → exclude for the whole style or condition, as `excludeStyles: ["financial"]` does. A154 then keeps it out of coverage, so peers are scored on the same basis.
  3. **Real but distorted by the denominator** (negative or tiny invested capital or equity) → use a robust formula. Greenblatt return on capital = EBIT / (net PP&E + net working capital), floored at a small positive denominator. FTNT/MANH/MEDP then keep their top quality rank.
  4. **Real extreme** (true 25 % FCF yield, true 5× P/B compounder) → keep it. The winsorise/z step already limits its pull.
- Deliverable: a table of every cell f1cb014c cleared or derived (ticker, field, old, new, class 1–4), with counts per class. Re-run fund-1.2.0 with class 3/4 cells restored and show the before/after ret15 IC and top-decile names.
