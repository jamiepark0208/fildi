# A195: [quant] Study — cointegration divergence as a put-selling entry signal

**From → to:** Muse → Claude · **Date:** 2026-10-19 · **Status:** open
**Source:** Reel by @josholdmixon (Jamie sent 10-20): pair trading explainer — cointegration ≠ correlation; enter on divergence, profit on mean reversion. His claim "nearly half of hedge funds use it" is unverified marketing framing; the underlying method is standard stat-arb.

## Thesis
Jamie sells puts directionally. The fildi-native version of pair trading is not equity pairs (needs borrow, two-sided infra) — it's **cointegration as an entry filter**: when a name in a cointegrated pair diverges DOWN vs its partner, its IV typically spikes and the spread historically reverts. Selling puts on the diverged leg = mean-reversion trade + premium.

## Study design (pre-registered, study before any build — A51 pattern)
- Universe: A148 peer groups + watchlist names with ≥2y daily bars (Schwab/Yahoo in DB).
- Step 1: Engle-Granger / Johansen screen within peer groups; keep pairs cointegrated at p<0.05 with spread half-life 5–60 days.
- Step 2: Signal days = spread z-score ≤ −2 on one leg. Compare put-selling outcomes (S003 reconstructed puts harness) on signal days vs all days for the same names: premium kept, assignment rate, worst 5-day underlying move.
- Pass: signal-day premium-kept improves ≥10% vs baseline with no worse tail (worst-decile loss not larger). Fail on either → kill, no build.
- Era split required (pre/post 2024) per A131 checklist.

## Expected result
Cointegration in large-cap peers is heavily arbitraged; base rate says effect is small. The IV-spike channel is the only plausible edge. If pass, output = a scanner flag ("diverged vs pair") on Breakdown, not an auto-trade.
