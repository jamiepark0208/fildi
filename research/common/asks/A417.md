# A417: Roll/defend analyzer ignores prior-roll credits — assign basis overstates cost on every rolled put (Medium)

**From → to:** Muse → Claude → Cursor · **Date:** 2026-11-23 · **Status:** done
**Dupe check:** searched ASKS.md for roll / assign / basis rows — A392 owns the analyzer itself and records this gap as standing in its build note + Claude review 9f03fbc0; A372 owns book-level accounting (E/C/D/P/F cash contract, account-level, shipped Nov 11) and does not track per-contract roll history. No row tracks roll credits per contract.
**Moves:** ops

## Thesis

The roll/defend analyzer (A392, north-star gap #1) is the tool Jamie uses to decide roll vs assign vs close on open short puts — and its assign basis is wrong on every put that has ever been rolled. `scripts/watchlist_sync/put_manage.py:build_options` computes `basis = strike − credit` where `credit` is only the current position's avg premium (from `portfolio_options`). Credits collected on prior rolls are nowhere in the number, even though A392's own spec says assign = "effective cost basis = strike − total credits collected (incl. prior rolls if visible in portfolio_orders)". A rolled put's true cost is lower by the roll credits, so assign looks worse than it is and the analyzer is biased toward rolling again — the exact decision it exists to inform, on real money, in the week the book resumes premium after a 7-day washout.

## Why now

- The analyzer is live in Portfolio Alerts (A392, Nov 10) and the book is being actively managed through the Nov selloff — rolled single-name puts are the positions being decided on right now.
- No new data source needed: `put_manage.py` already queries `portfolio_orders` filled put fills (sell-to-open / buy-to-close) for entry dating in the same file — the roll chain is one summation away.
- A372's accounting contract (§1, shipped Nov 11) is account-level cash; it does not track per-contract roll history, so nothing else will close this gap.

## What changes

1. Per open short put, sum the net credits from its roll chain: buy-to-close + sell-to-open fill pairs in `portfolio_orders` for the same account/symbol, from the position's first open to today. Assign basis becomes strike − (original credit + prior roll credits); hold break-even moves with it. Roll candidates' break-even already uses original credit + net roll credit (Claude review 9f03fbc0) — keep the two consistent.
2. Show the roll-credit component as its own line in the analyzer output ("prior roll credits $X"), so Jamie can see what moved the basis.
3. Linkage rule (the one judgment call — Claude to bless in review before Cursor builds): define what counts as a roll vs a close + fresh open (same-day pair, adjacent expiry, or an explicit roll marker if the fills carry one). If the fills cannot support a defensible linkage, display "roll credits unknown" for that contract — never invent a number.

## Acceptance

- Fixture with a rolled put (real book example if one exists, else synthetic fills): assign basis = strike − (original credit + roll credits), to the cent; the roll-credit line shows the summed credits.
- A put never rolled is byte-identical to today (basis, break-even, close P&L unchanged).
- A contract whose fills cannot link a roll shows "roll credits unknown" and keeps today's basis, flagged, not silently adjusted.
- `put_manage.py` tests pass; no change to the chain-ladder call, no new tables, no new model.

## Risks

- Roll linkage ambiguity is real: a buy-to-close followed by a sell-to-open is sometimes a close + fresh trade, not a roll. A wrong heuristic mis-states the basis in the other direction. Mitigation: Claude reviews the linkage rule first; the "unknown" fallback bounds the damage.
- `portfolio_orders` may not carry enough linkage signal (no roll id). Then the honest outcome is the "unknown" display + a note on what field would fix it — still better than today's silent understatement.

## Cost

Quota/tokens: one query + arithmetic in `put_manage.py`, one UI line. Complexity: low — no new tables, no model, no live-money path change (advice/display only). Review: one Claude [quant] pass on the linkage rule, then Cursor builds.
