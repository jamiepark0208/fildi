## Research headline — ranked menu
1. **Sector-laggard catch-up** — Jamie's example is the best-documented effect (Hou 2007 intra-
   industry big→small lead-lag, weeks timescale, strongest in small/neglected names, asymmetric
   toward slow diffusion of bad news). Implementable on OHLCV: z-score of stock vs sector ETF,
   |z|≥2, 1–4 week hold.
2. **IV−RV spread** — the structural vol risk premium, base edge of all premium selling. Screen
   single names, not the index (index short-dated premium compressed near record lows).
3. **Post-earnings drift tailwind** — sell puts 2–30d after positive surprises (~60-day drift).
   Never hold short premium THROUGH the binary (fat tails).
4. **1-week / 1-month reversal** — liquidity-provision mechanism, capacity-limited, suits small
   accounts. Mirror image for call-selling side.
5. **Residual momentum** (sector-neutralized ranking) — moderate confidence, contested post-2015.
6. **Gamma-flip regime filter** — sell puts above the flip (dealer damping), stand aside below.
   Mechanism strong, return evidence thin.
7. **Overnight/intraday decomposition** — prefer laggards whose lag is overnight-gap-driven.
- **Decayed — do not build**: index-rebalance pinning (S&P addition effect 7.4% → 0.3%);
  intraday VWAP reversion as selection edge (HFT-competed); assuming earnings implied moves are
  systematically overpriced (name-specific).
- **Laggard-vs-broken filter stack** (the key risk): reject on negative 3M EPS revisions,
  top-decile/rising short interest, lag on >2× abnormal volume, single-day >5% gap on company
  news, Piotroski F < 4, price <$5. Accept only flat/positive revisions, low stable SI, lag on
  normal volume, no company news, ≥60% sector participation.
- **Options mapping**: Leland's result — a premium seller must believe in mean reversion — so
  equity mean-reversion signals stacked with short premium give two independent reasons for one
  outcome. Highest-conviction put sale: sector laggard + rich IV + clean revisions + above
  gamma flip + post-crush timing.
