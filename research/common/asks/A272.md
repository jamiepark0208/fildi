- **Cover:** "4 Advanced QUANT PROJECTS to build in college."
- **Project 1 — Marchenko-Pastur on a real stock correlation matrix.**
  Pull daily returns for a few hundred stocks, build the correlation
  matrix, plot eigenvalues against the MP density. Eigenvalues inside
  [λ−, λ+] can't be told apart from noise; only the few above λ+ carry
  real structure (largest = market factor). λ± = σ²(1 ± √q)², q = N/T.
  Clean by flattening noise eigenvalues and keeping signal ones, build a
  minimum-variance portfolio from raw and cleaned matrices, compare out
  of sample. "The gap between the two is what trusting noise costs."
- **Project 2 — Replace a fixed hedge ratio with a Kalman Filter.**
  Static OLS hedge ratio assumes the relationship between two stocks never
  changes; it drifts. Treat β as a hidden random-walk state, Kalman filter
  updates it daily (y_t = β_t x_t + ε_t; β_t = β_{t−1} + w_t; Kalman gain
  K_t = P x / (x²P + R)). The spread trade is the filter's prediction
  error; dividing by its std gives a live z-score for entries/exits.
  Process noise Q is the knob (too high → β chases noise; too low → OLS
  again). Backtest against a rolling OLS hedge on the same pair, OOS.
- **Project 3 — Simulate Avellaneda-Stoikov market making, watch inventory
  risk.** A market maker earns the spread but every fill changes inventory,
  and inventory is risk. Quote around a reservation price instead of mid:
  long → both quotes shift down (sell more easily); short → shift up.
  Spread widens with volatility and with time left in the session.
  r(s,t) = s − qγσ²(T−t); δ^a + δ^b = γσ²(T−t) + (2/γ)ln(1 + γ/k).
  Simulate a Brownian mid price with Poisson order arrivals whose intensity
  falls as you quote further from mid; run against a naive symmetric quote
  and compare profit and inventory. The paper's result: inventory-aware
  quoting reduces the spread of outcomes at a small cost in average profit.
  "Check that your simulation agrees before you believe it."
- **Project 4 — Solve Almgren-Chriss, find the cheapest way to sell a large
  position.** Selling at once moves price against you; selling slowly
  leaves you exposed while you wait. Minimize expected cost + λ × variance
  of cost (λ = risk aversion). With linear temporary impact the optimal
  schedule has closed form: x(t) = X sinh(κ(T−t))/sinh(κT),
  κ = √(λσ²/η). A patient trader sells almost evenly, a nervous one sells
  early. Plot trajectories for several λ plus the efficient frontier of
  cost vs variance. Then swap in square-root impact (closer to real data)
  and see how the schedule changes. "The model's linear impact is a
  simplification, and finding where it breaks is the useful part."

  raw). The slide's claim reproduces.
- **Kalman: PASS, marginal.** Synthetic drifting-β pair: Kalman β RMSE
  0.168 vs rolling-OLS (60d) 0.173. Small edge, not a blowout — the
  plan's pass bar therefore demands a win on REAL pairs OOS, not on this
  synthetic. Value here is the adaptive framework + live z-score, not a
  dramatic RMSE win.
- **AS: PASS, strong.** 200 simulated sessions each: inventory-aware
  quoting P&L std 0.55 vs naive symmetric 1.54 (nearly 3× narrower
  outcomes) at a mean-profit cost of 0.04. The paper's result reproduces
  exactly as the slide states it.
- **AC: PASS on shape, with a units trap.** The closed form's shape is
  governed by κT, not by λ alone: κT→0 gives linear TWAP (20% sold per
  fifth); κT=3 gives 45% sold in the first fifth. A first test using the
  paper's figure λ values with guessed σ/η units gave κT≈0.03 for every λ
  (all schedules linear) — so λ MUST be calibrated in fildi's own units
  (σ, η, X, T) until κT spans the interesting range. That calibration is
  a plan step, not an afterthought.

## 4. First-level analysis — what each method does for fildi

- **MP cleaning: the strongest fit.** Fildi already computes a correlation
  matrix for portfolio risk — and that computation is already flagged
  weak (H6/A257: misaligned pairing, abs weights). MP cleaning is the
  direct upgrade to that matrix: strip the noise eigenvalues before the
  matrix feeds VaR and the risk panel. It also gives the fund score a
  cleaner covariance if one is ever built. This is enhancement of an
  existing, flagged component, not a new system.
- **Kalman pairs: the weakest direct fit, honestly.** Fildi runs no pairs
  book. Its value is twofold: (a) a paper pairs arm, if [quant] rules a
  universe worth trading; (b) adaptive β tracking — a drifting exposure
  measure for Model List names vs SPY, which IS a fildi-native use. The
  plan does not pretend pairs is already a fildi strategy.
- **AS inventory quoting: a framework transfer, not a market-making
  business.** Fildi doesn't make markets. But selling puts IS earning a
  spread (premium) while accumulating inventory (short gamma, assignment
  risk). The reservation-price idea maps directly: when the book is heavy
  short in a name or sector, shift new sales (lower deltas, wider
  spreads) the way a market maker shifts quotes when long. The static
  0.09Δ rule (A250) is the fixed-quote version; AS makes it
  inventory-aware. Paper simulation only — no live quoting in this plan.
- **AC execution scheduling: the Thursday-close problem, formalized.**
  The book must close or roll ITM shorts by Thursday's close (Robinhood
  can auto-sell in the last 30 min of expiry day). AC gives a
  cost-vs-variance-optimal schedule for exiting a position across a
  window instead of all at once — for rolls/closes AND for liquidating
  assigned stock (the RGTI-style overhang). Paper first; the slide's own
  caveat (linear impact breaks) is built into the study via the
  square-root extension.

## 5. Second-level analysis — sequencing, interactions, risks

- **Build order is forced: A257 before MP.** Cleaning a misaligned

  AS stays a toy.
- **AC needs real impact.** Same logic: λ, σ, η must be calibrated in

  mandatory, not optional — the slide itself says finding where linear
  breaks is the useful part.
- **Interactions:** cleaned correlations (MP) → better portfolio-risk
  measurement → better inventory measurement (AS input) → better
  exit scheduling (AC input). The four methods compose into one
  risk-measurement chain; the plan builds them in that order.
- **No live-book rule changes in this plan.** Research + display + paper
  arms only. Any scorer, sizing, or quoting change needs a separate
  [quant] verdict + live-weeks evidence (A250 precedent).

## 6. Use cases (each: why + expected value + pass/fail bar)

### Marchenko-Pastur

- **UC1 — Cleaned correlation into portfolio-risk.ts VaR.**
  Why: the VaR input matrix is already flagged weak (H6). Expected
  value: a VaR that trusts less noise. Pass bar: on rolling windows,
  cleaned correlation is closer to next-window realized correlation
  (Frobenius) than raw on ≥60% of windows, AND VaR backtest (Kupiec)

- **UC2 — Cleaned min-variance portfolio (the slide's own test).**

  vol than raw min-var on the same universe, or kill.

  eigenvalues above λ+ measures how concentrated the market factor is.
  Expected value: a regime flag in the risk panel. Pass bar: series
  computed nightly for the Model List universe, display-only; no rule
  attached.

### Avellaneda-Stoikov

### Almgren-Chriss

- **UC1 — Close/roll scheduling ahead of Thursday close.**
  Why: the book's hardest recurring execution problem, currently

- **UC2 — Assigned-stock liquidation schedule.**
  Why: assignment overhang (RGTI-style) needs a selling schedule, not a
