## 1st-level analysis: why this matters to fildi

Fildi sells American-style equity puts. A European Black-Scholes price understates an American put by the early-exercise premium — on the sheet's own example, 5.5735 vs ~6.09, a ~0.52 (~9%) gap. Any fair-value, put-edge, or backtest-pricing path that leans on European BS carries that bias on every American name. The three American methods on the sheet converge (~6.089 binomial/FD; LSM 6.04, low as its own bias note predicts), so the sheet is internally consistent textbook material, not a novel claim — no tool to test, nothing institution-gated. Value here is a reference pricer, not a strategy.

## 2nd-level analysis: what is actually new vs fildi

Fildi already computes fair values (Oct 22: yield percent→decimal normalization fixed a fair-value collapse; automation work references FV jump filters), and prices live chains from Schwab. What fildi does not have, as far as this row can establish, is an independent model pricer to validate chain mids and fair values against — a second opinion that flags a stale or broken quote instead of trusting the chain. That is the build: a small validation pricer, not a pricing replacement. Live quotes stay primary; the model is the check.

## Use cases (why, expected value)

1. **Chain-mid validation flag.** Price American puts with binomial (n >= 500, Richardson) + FD cross-check; flag names where model and chain mid diverge beyond tolerance. Why: a stale/broken chain mid currently flows straight into edge and fair value. Expected value: bad-quote flags before they become bad edges.
2. **American-vs-European bias audit on put edge.** Recompute put-edge inputs with the American pricer and measure how much edge the European approximation was giving away or overstating. Why: the ~9% example gap is large relative to put-selling margins. Expected value: a quantified bias number, and a corrected edge where it matters.
3. **Backtest pricing leg for A246.** The A246 closed loop needs a pricer for generated candidates; a validated American pricer is its pricing dependency. Why: backtests priced on European BS misstate American-put payoffs. Expected value: A246's backtests stop carrying the early-exercise bias.
