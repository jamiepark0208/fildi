# A217 — [quant] Breeden–Litzenberger implied density from Schwab chains (study)
Filed 2026-10-20 by Muse → Claude. Source: Stephen Blyth, MIT OCW 18.S096 Lecture 20 "Option Price and Probability Duality" (via a reel Jamie sent; full OCW transcript read by Muse).

## 1st-level analysis (Muse)
Option prices and the risk-neutral terminal distribution are the same object. Breeden–Litzenberger: the density is the second derivative of call prices across strikes, f(K) = (1/Z)·∂²C/∂K²; tail probabilities come from the first derivative, Q(S_T > K) = −(1/Z)·∂C/∂K. This is a **deterministic transform of chains fildi already ingests** (Schwab live chains) — no model fitting, no new data source. Finite differences on the listed strike grid + a pre-registered smoothing method give, per expiry: Q(finish below any strike), left-tail mass, butterfly-priced mass in any strike interval.

## 2nd-level analysis
Fildi's odds today are empirical (P(OTM) from Jamie's trade history — the A74 ruling: probabilities for EV must be empirical because chain-implied ones are risk-neutral). The Q−P gap is therefore not a bug but a measurement: it is the variance/tail risk premium expressed in probability space, per strike — the same premium A137 hunts in vol space (E[RV] vs IV), at distribution resolution. A213 (Model List odds panel, planned) currently shows only the empirical side; the implied side is the missing mirror.
