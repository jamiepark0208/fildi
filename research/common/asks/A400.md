### 2nd-level analysis (what it means for fildi)
- The FF leg is an honesty filter for the fund score: a name's "alpha" that vanishes under FF5+MOM is factor beta, not skill. That is the same governed-testing discipline as A352/A387 (grade on realized P&L, not rank IC).
- The regime leg is the missing producer for A365's event-stack guard, which today has no regime input — it warns on backwardation + events but cannot tell calm from stressed.
- LSM prices the exact risk a put-seller carries: early assignment. The American-minus-European premium IS the assignment-risk price, and fildi currently has no American pricer (only BS P(ITM) in put_manage.py).
- Heston is the only leg that could replace BS IV with a smile-aware vol — but calibration multi-modality is the known failure mode, so it stays study-only until the stability bar passes.

### Use cases (with why + expected value)
1. **FF attribution panel per watchlist name (build if leg passes).** Regress each name's excess returns on FF5+MOM (Kenneth French data, free). Report alpha, Newey-West t-stat, and factor betas next to the fund score. Why: kills false "alpha" in rankings. Expected value: measurable — share of top-decile fund-score names whose alpha is insignificant (t < 2) after FF5; if that share is large, the score's stock-picking claim weakens and the UI must say so.
2. **HMM regime service feeding A365 + put manager (build if leg passes).** Daily filtered regime probability (calm/stressed) from SPY + watchlist returns; filtered, never smoothed (the slide's look-ahead trap, and the same discipline as S-series walk-forward rules). Why: the event-stack guard gains a regime gate — no new short premium when P(stressed) is high. Expected value: fewer short-premium entries into stressed regimes; measured as entries avoided + their subsequent 5-day P&L vs entries taken.
3. **LSM American pricer for short puts (build if leg passes).** Price every open short put American vs European; surface the early-exercise premium as assignment risk in the put manager. Why: assignment is the put-seller's tail event and today it has no price. Expected value: assignment-risk dollars per position, visible before expiry week; measured against realized early assignments.
4. **Heston IV surface (study only, build only if stability bar passes).** Calibrate to live Schwab chains; compare Heston IV vs BS IV across strikes. Why: wing pricing for far-OTM short puts is where BS IV is most wrong. Expected value: if Heston IV beats BS IV out-of-sample on 5 chains (RMSE bar below), put_edge's wing estimates improve; if not, DROP with the reason recorded — no limbo.
