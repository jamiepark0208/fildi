## What the research says (Claude 10-22, web + our studies)
| Source | Finding | Use here |
|---|---|---|
| Cremers & Weinbaum (JFQA 2010) | stocks with expensive calls vs puts (call−put IV spread) beat expensive-put stocks by ~50 bp/week | direction: high spread → long call, low → long put |
| Xing, Zhang & Zhao (2008) | steep put skew (OTM put IV − ATM call IV) → bad news, lower future returns | direction: steep skew → long put; and avoid long calls on steep skew |
| Short-term reversal + capitulation (Diodato 2019 CMT; StockCharts breadth stages) | panic lows (breadth washout, volume spike, < 20 % of names above 200d) → strong 5–20 d rebounds | timing for long calls: oversold + washout, entry on the first reversal day, not the falling knife |
| IV vs realized vol (practitioner; tastytrade SPY: RV > IV only 18 % of the time) | buyers win only when realized move > implied; "cheap" IV after a shock can still crush | price filter: buy only when IV ≤ recent RV *and* IV rank is low, so vega helps, not hurts |
| Our S076 | unconditional long call +14 %/trade t 1.1 (bull tape); long put fails | baseline to beat; confirms timing + price filter are needed |
| Our S018/S020 | long straddle fails | don't buy vol without direction |

## Strategy to test (merged): Direction × Timing × Price
1. **Direction score** (per stock, daily): call−put IV spread (Cremers–Weinbaum), put skew (Xing–Zhang–Zhao), trend (price vs 50/200d), existing Fildi tech score / fit. Long-call side uses the same signals the sell-puts side trusts (bullish); long-put side uses the sell-calls / trim-risk / avoid signals (bearish).
2. **Timing (the "low/high" finder):** long calls → oversold reversal (RSI(2) < 10 or 20d drop ≥ 1.5 σ, then a close above the prior day's high) with a market washout bonus (breadth < 20 % above 200d, VIX spike fading). Long puts → failed rally / breakdown (close below the 50d after a lower high, rising put skew), market stretched (breadth > 80 %, VIX near lows).
3. **Price filter (buyer's version of "rich premium"):** IV30 ≤ RV20 and IV rank < 50 → the option is cheap vs how the stock actually moves. The expected move is measured from the current price: required move to break even = (strike − spot + premium)/spot for calls, vs the stock's own distribution of 10–30 d moves (same history-based EV engine as S076 — no lognormal).
4. **Contract choice:** delta 0.40–0.60, 30–60 DTE (less theta than weeklies); exits at +50 % / +100 %, a −50 % stop, or 21 DTE. Earnings excluded unless the study shows otherwise.

## Why these choices
- Three legs, because each paper explains one failure of naive option buying: wrong direction (skew/spread), wrong timing (reversal), overpaying (IV vs RV). S076 lost money on long puts exactly because it had none of the three.
- Holdout + t ≥ 2 + both eras: one bull run can make any long-call rule look good (S076's +14 % was mostly 2024→).
- Comparing to buying the stock: if the stock signal earns the same per $ risked, the option only adds decay.
