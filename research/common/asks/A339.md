## 1. Goal

Test the reel's "Triple RSI" pullback strategy on SPY, honestly: reproduce its backtest on fildi golden-source data, run it head-to-head against the RSI(2) Connors variant that A184 item 7 already queues, and — the fildi question — test it as a **sell-put timing signal** on the put-pnl harness. Adopt only if it earns a timing job; the long-only edge alone is not enough, because it underperforms buy-and-hold by an order of magnitude.

Rules as stated in the reel (transcript verified by Muse, 187s reel): 5-day RSI below 30, RSI declined three days in a row, RSI three days ago below 60, close above the 200-day moving average; sell when 5-day RSI crosses above 50. Trades last a few days; the reel claims 83 trades from 1993 to today, ~90% win rate, ~1.4% average gain, ~5-day holds, "almost linear" equity growth.

## 2. Muse 1st-level analysis

The claim is mostly real but the framing is misleading in the exact way that matters. Muse reproduced it on the VM (Yahoo adjusted daily SPY, 1993-01-29 → 2026-11-04, 8,500 bars, Wilder RSI(5)): next-open entry / close exit gives 91 trades, 83.5% win, +1.32% avg, 7.2-day holds, +222% total, max drawdown −6.4%; close-to-close gives 92 trades, 89.1% win, +1.32% avg — essentially the reel's numbers (83 trades / 90% / ~1.4% / ~5 days; the small count gap is the exact decline-rule reading or data vintage). The forward-5-day return after a signal day is +0.88% mean, 68.0% positive, vs a +0.23% / 58.6% base rate — a real edge, ~3.8x the base mean. But the strategy sits in cash ~92% of the time: +222% total vs +2,853% buy-and-hold (10.8% CAGR) over the same span. A 90% win rate on a strategy that returns 1/13th of buy-and-hold is not a strategy to run long-only. It is a timing signal looking for a job — and the job in fildi is timing short puts into a bounce, not buying dips outright.

## 3. Muse 2nd-level analysis

- **Already-covered check (verified in code/index):** RSI mean reversion on index ETFs is A184 item 7 ("RSI(2) short-term mean reversion on index ETFs (Connors)" — Tier 2, daily data, open, unbuilt). This row implements that item with the reel's named RSI(5) parameterization and runs the RSI(2) head-to-head, so A184 item 7 gets closed with evidence either way. An RSI(14) indicator already exists in-repo (`lib/indicators/src/rsi.ts`, TradingView built-in port, 5m/15m/1h) — the study needs a parameterized RSI(5) + decline-count variant, not a new indicator. S025 found no bounce in single stocks, so this is index-only (SPY, plus QQQ as a second index); no single-name application without its own test.
- **The edge is decaying.** Split sample (same rules, next-open entry): 1993–2014 gives 55 trades, 85.5% win, +1.60% avg; 2015–2026 gives 36 trades, 80.6% win, +0.89% avg. The win rate holds up; the payoff per trade nearly halved. Any adoption must be sized on the recent subsample, not the 33-year average — and must be re-graded on a schedule, not assumed permanent.
- **The failure mode is the 2011 trade.** Worst trade in the reproduction: −6.4% (Jul 29 → Aug 15, 2011, debt-ceiling drawdown), then −4.8% (Feb–Mar 2025). The signal fires into pullbacks that keep falling. For a put-seller that is the exact risk: selling premium into a signal that catches a falling knife. The put-harness test must report the left tail (worst trades, max drawdown), not just the win rate.
- **Execution assumption moves the win rate 6 points.** Close-to-close entry gives 89.1% win; next-open entry gives 83.5% — same rules, same data. The reel never states its execution. The golden-source reproduction must pre-register the execution (next open is the honest one) and report the close-to-close variant as a sensitivity, not the headline.

## 4. Use cases (concrete, with why + expected value)

- **UC1 — Sell-put timing (the fildi job).** Signal fires (RSI(5) < 30 after three declines, above the 200-day MA) → sell short puts (30–45 DTE, standard deltas) into the expected bounce, on the put-pnl harness, vs a random-timing control and vs S151's two passing signals. Expected value: a third passing timing signal raises put-entry quality — the bounce edge (+0.88% forward 5d vs +0.23% base) is exactly what a short put wants; even a fail kills a plausible prior with evidence and stops anyone from hand-trading the reel's 90% claim.
- **UC2 — Dip-bounce timing check for the Model List.** When the Model List flags a name in a pullback, the triple-RSI signal on SPY (or the name's index) confirms or denies the bounce before capital goes in. Expected value: fewer entries into falling knives — the 2011/2025 left-tail trades are the concrete failure the check must catch.
- **UC3 — Close A184 item 7 with evidence.** The backlog item (RSI(2) Connors on index ETFs) gets its concrete test, with the triple-RSI(5) as the named parameterization and a head-to-head on the same harness, same costs, same holdout. Expected value: the queue stops carrying an untested line — the winner is named, the loser is recorded as failed for this use, and the next agent does not re-litigate it.

## 8. Pass / fail bars

- **P1 (reproduction):** golden-source run lands within tolerance of the VM reproduction (trades 80–95, win 80–90%, avg +1.0–1.6%, maxDD better than −10%) or each miss is explained. Unexplained disagreement beyond tolerance → STOP, close as failed validation.
- **P2 (timing earns its job):** on the put-pnl harness, signal-timed put entries beat the random-timing control by ≥1.0% annualized on harness pnl OR ≥5pp win-rate uplift, with worst-trade and maxDD reported. Failing P2 = drop as a timing input (the long-only edge alone never adopts — it returns ~1/13th of buy-and-hold).
- **P3 (head-to-head):** RSI(2) vs RSI(5) reported on identical metrics; winner named, loser recorded. Missing the comparison = incomplete.
- **P4 (decay honest):** recent-subsample (2015+) results reported separately; if the recent subsample fails P2 while the full sample passes, verdict is drop-or-shadow, never adopt-on-full-sample.
- **P5 (no live change):** no live score-input or live-money rule change from this row under any outcome; adoption is research-lane/shadow first. Violation = STOP, close.
