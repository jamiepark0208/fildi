## Why it matters
This is Jamie's calibration philosophy as infrastructure: typed, calibrated
probabilities instead of guesses — the same idea as A85 (Brier-scored pundit
tracker) and Pythia (A124). A decision server that answers in milliseconds from
model probabilities (not generated text) could score put-selling setups, pattern
signals (A120), and pundit claims with honest confidence numbers.

## Proposal (Cursor pilots on Jamie's Mac)
1. Stand up OpenJev locally via MLX (Apple silicon) or use the free Codiv hosted
   tier for the pilot — no GPU purchase, no card.
2. Pilot: wire 5–10 recurring fildi judgments through it as typed questions
   (e.g., "setup quality: high/med/low", "earnings blowup risk: yes/no") alongside
   the existing heuristic scores.
3. Score its probabilities with Brier scores against realized outcomes over 2–4 weeks
   (same discipline as A85/A124).
4. Decision gate: adopt as a signal-scoring layer only if calibrated (Brier beats
   the heuristic baseline).

- **Muse 10-17 — use-case analysis (not a plan; written to sync with Claude's lab work, no overlap: the lab tests question *forms*, this tests task *framing* and *interpretation*).**
  - **Why powerful-but-noisy isn't a contradiction:** OpenJev's power is the *mechanism* (ms typed probability reads from logits, no text parsing, schema-safe). The noise is the *oracle*: a 26B open model. Logit-reading reports the weak model's beliefs honestly and fast — it doesn't make them smarter. Two more noise sources: raw LLM logits are systematically overconfident (Claude measured 81/128 extreme — textbook miscalibration, fixable post-hoc), and the headline benchmark asks OpenJev to beat the options market at pricing puts. No 26B model wins that fight; that test can only produce noise.
  - **The wrong benchmark:** grading put-OTM vs market-implied odds tests whether OpenJev *replaces* the market. The right question is whether it *adds information on top of* the market. Keep put-OTM in the ledger as a forecaster, but judge OpenJev on question types where fildi's proprietary data is the edge and the baseline is a base rate, not a market price: score_disagree (type 7), rec quality (type 2), risk-cause (type 8). The ablation already shows the model reads our scores (0.21 shift, biggest mover) — lean into that.
  - **Tests proposed:**
    1. **Deviation framing:** instead of "P(OTM)?", ask "market says 73%; given OUR scores, is true P higher or lower?" Score only the sign of the deviation vs realized outcomes. Easier task for a weak model; measures added information, not replacement.
    2. **Ensemble:** logit-average OpenJev's p with market odds; test whether the blend beats market alone on resolved puts. Standard use of a weak forecaster — uncorrelated errors help even when it loses solo.
    3. **Calibration layer:** once ≥50 resolved per type, fit Platt scaling (or isotonic) on the ledger, then re-score Brier. Directly attacks the overconfidence finding. v3-range removing extremes is already evidence the noise is calibration, not content.
    4. **Lean state:** ablation says weights/fair_value/guidance/earnings are noise for short horizons (≤0.09 shift); scores/market/street/options move answers (0.11–0.21). Test dropping the noise groups — fewer tokens, less prompt sensitivity, maybe better calibration.
    5. **Position-bias fix verification:** order-flip averaging identified — verify on held-out blank A/B prompts that the flipped average converges to ~0.5 before trusting any choice question.
  - **Suggested kill:** stop using put-OTM-vs-market-IV as the pass/fail headline. A model that can't beat the market solo can still earn its keep as a deviation signal + ensemble member on proprietary-edge questions.
