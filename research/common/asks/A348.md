## 1. The 7 strategies (as printed)

1. **Pairs trading divergence** — two related stocks that usually move together; when the gap gets unusually wide, long the weaker, short the stronger, bet on convergence.
2. **Volatility compression breakout** — price in a very tight range (Bollinger Bands chart, no values printed); low vol leads to a strong move; enter on the breakout with momentum.
3. **Post-earnings drift (PEAD)** — stocks keep moving after an earnings surprise, in the same direction, for days or weeks. (Chart: abnormal BHAR by news decile, weeks 1–13.)
4. **Intraday gap continuation** — stocks opening sharply higher/lower than the prior close; if early momentum supports the gap, trade the same direction intraday.
5. **Z-score mean reversion** — price stretched far from its average (±2 on the chart, 2010–2024); position for the move back to the mean.
6. **RSI rotation momentum** — rotate into stocks with strong RSI readings; ride relative strength instead of buying weak stocks. (Chart: strategy vs S&P, log scale, 1992–2024.)
7. **Close-to-open edge** — the return from one day's close to the next day's open; whether holding overnight beats intraday trading. (Diagram: entry, MFE, MAE, exit — an edge-ratio frame.)

## 4. Muse's 1st-level analysis

The carousel is a list of textbook ideas with no numbers — the value is in the screen, not the slides. The screen says: one real edge (overnight), one modest edge (z-score long), one asymmetric maybe (gaps), one dead simple version (squeeze), one already dead (pairs), two that need fildi data (PEAD, RSI rotation). The overnight edge is the one that matters to Jamie specifically: a put seller's premium is, in large part, compensation for overnight gap risk — if 93% of the market's return accrues overnight, the risk he is paid to take is the whole game, and the study should quantify the gap tail, not just the mean. The z-score result is useful only as a head-to-head: A339 already carries the RSI version of index mean reversion, so a z-score row that doesn't beat RSI(2)/triple-RSI on the harness is noise.

## 5. Muse's 2nd-level analysis

Three of these legs touch the same nerve: **when does the market actually move?** Overnight (edge), after earnings (PEAD), after gaps (continuation/reversal). For a put-selling book the practical question is not "can I trade these" — Jamie doesn't trade equities intraday — it is "what is the overnight/earnings/gap risk priced into the premium I collect, and where is it mispriced?" That frames the study as risk-pricing work, not strategy-collecting: the overnight leg quantifies the gap tail the book is exposed to; PEAD quantifies the post-earnings drift the book sells into; the gap leg quantifies whether gap-down mornings (the book's worst mornings) continue or bounce. The RSI-rotation leg is the only pure alpha-seeking leg, and it earns its place only in the put-filter form.
