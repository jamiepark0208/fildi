# A100 — Free compute sweep backend (Colab/Kaggle for parallel backtest sweeps)

**Status:** declined
**From:** Muse → Cursor
**Date:** 2026-10-14
**Severity:** low-medium

## Context
Jamie asked (Oct 14), pushing back on the "no model training" verdict: why not integrate free GPU
setups (Colab, Kaggle)? Honest answer recorded here: full model training (gradient descent) is not
the bottleneck — data is. The Oct-6 study proved learned weights have nothing to learn on 1,513
clustered trades, and A89's evolution lab was declined as a data-snooping machine. Free GPUs don't
fix a data problem. But embarrassingly parallel backtest sweeps ARE worth free compute.

## What to build
1. **Sweep runner**: parameter grids over the existing backtest harnesses (challenger params from
   A86/A92, indicator thresholds from A93) — each cell independent, runnable anywhere.
2. **Free backends**: Google Colab (free T4) and Kaggle (free weekly GPU hours) notebooks that pull
   the repo's backtest scripts + a parameter slice, run, and write results back (CSV to Drive or
   artifact). No paid tier, no sfcompute.
3. **Result aggregation**: combine sweep cells into the walk-forward + Diebold-Mariano comparisons
   the quant rows already require — compute is the only thing being outsourced.
4. **Guardrails**: sweeps test pre-registered hypotheses only (no fishing); the anti-overfitting
   rules from A86/A89 apply (train/val/test, deflated Sharpe awareness at small samples).

## What's deliberately NOT in this plan
- Training neural nets / fine-tuning LLMs on the trade history — data too small and clustered;
  Oct-6 verdict stands. Revisit only with ≥3y of point-in-time option outcomes (A89's condition).

## Acceptance
- [ ] A parameter sweep runs end-to-end on Colab free tier with results aggregated locally.
- [ ] No paid compute; total cost $0.
- [ ] Sweeps feed the existing quant review gates, not around them.
