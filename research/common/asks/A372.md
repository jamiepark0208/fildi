#### 3. When “enough profit” becomes a reason to close

At every eligible review, compare **remaining** payoff from hold vs close/partial/roll and the portfolio effect. Entry premium remains in lifetime P&L but is sunk for the same current state's prospective comparison. Profit-captured percentage and unusually fast profit are review triggers, not the sole answer.

For one short option, holding instead of buying back now has incremental economic value roughly:
`current executable buyback − expected future exit/settlement liability − extra costs/financing + collateral carry difference`.
Evaluate the full loss distribution and stressed account, not just its average. If an estimated distribution is unavailable/uncalibrated, show remaining premium, realistic close cost and explicit scenarios; do not invent odds. Constraint-driven reductions can be sensible even when expected holding profit remains positive. State “pay $X / forgo $Y expected income to release $Z capacity and reduce this stress loss.”

Worked arithmetic fixture (hypothetical, not an IREN recommendation): standard50P sold for$1.50, now executable buyback$0.15. Closing locks$135 gross; holding can add at most$15 gross. If expiry stock is45, liability is$500 and holding rather than closing loses an additional$485. That is not an estimate of the event probability, but it makes the remaining reward vs downside concrete. Fees, cash yield, taxes and actual remaining days are separate. If two contracts exist, compare closing one and keeping one; with one contract do not offer a fractional close.

Actions and discipline:
- **Take profit / reduce:** remaining reward is unattractive for the risk and capital tie-up, a precommitted target is met with adverse evidence, or reserve/portfolio concentration warrants reduction. State which reason; no “greedy” moral label.
- **Hold:** adequate remaining compensation, thesis intact, account can tolerate assignment/downside; retain an invalidation condition and next review trigger.
- **Risk exit:** thesis invalidated, funding/margin capacity impaired, or whole-book loss exceeds chosen tolerance. Apply to losers as well as winners; never “hold until back to entry” as an exit policy.
- **Roll:** explicit close plus a separately eligible new position, preserving realized loss and extra duration. A net credit is not profit or a repaired thesis. Re-run reserve/correlation tests; compare cash after closing with keeping/rolling.
- **Accept assignment:** only if the resulting shares/sector exposure and funding are intended and feasible; not because realizing a loss feels worse.
- **Take profit and retain cash** must be a first-class output. “Re-sell” is a separate suggestion subject to the current reserve band, never an automatic continuation.

Stop discipline is distinct from stop-order execution. Model gap/slippage and liquidity; a stop price is not a guaranteed fill. No automatic orders in this task. Define review/exit conditions before entry, including loss budget, thesis/event invalidation and time limit; do not bless a universal2× option-premium stop because it sounds disciplined. For existing positions reconstruct the original thesis if documented; otherwise label the plan as created today, not retroactively precommitted. Covered calls/long options/stock holdings need their own lifecycle logic and net exposure accounting; closing a covered call alone does not reduce stock downside.
