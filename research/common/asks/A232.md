## 1st-level analysis — why transfer should exist (Muse)
Three distinct channels, and they must not be blended into one "spillover" number:
1. **Shared end-demand.** Peers sell into the same demand pool. A leader's guide is new information about the pool, not just the firm. (NFLX's guide cut is a streaming-demand fact before it is a Netflix fact.)
2. **Supplier/customer links.** One firm's capex or orders are literally another firm's revenue. META raising AI capex by $135–145B is a revenue statement for NVDA/AVGO/ANET/VRT/MU — the strongest channel because the dollars are the same dollars.
3. **Sector multiple re-rating.** A leader's print reprices the whole group's multiple (the market re-marks what a unit of sector earnings is worth) even where fundamentals barely transfer. This channel is sentiment, decays fastest, and is the one most likely to invert on positioning.

**Empirical record (what the literature supports, at the level this study needs):** earnings information transfer to rival firms is documented back to Foster (1981) and repeatedly since — rivals of an announcing firm show significant abnormal returns around the leader's announcement, on the order of ~0.5–1.5% for close same-industry rivals on big surprises, with the effect concentrated in the announcement window and the following **1–5 trading days**, then decaying rather than compounding. Analysts also revise *peer* forecasts in the direction of the leader's surprise (revision transfer), and supply-chain momentum (a firm's returns predicting its customers'/suppliers' later returns, Menzly–Ozbas line of work) is a separate, slower effect. These are priors, not fildi numbers — the study below measures all of it in fildi's own ~570-name universe before anything consumes it.

**Tonight's worked examples (Oct 21, 2026 — motivating cases, not evidence):**
- META +3% AH: revenue beat, Q4 guide raised, AI capex +$135–145B → channel 2 transfer to NVDA/AVGO/ANET/VRT/MU before any of them report.
- SMCI +9.55%: revenue guide raised to $18.5–19.5B → channel 1/2 confirmation for the AI server chain (NVDA, DELL, HPE).
- T −6.45% on a guide cut → channel 1 question for VZ/TMUS: sector demand, or T-specific (fiber spend, churn)? The study's job is exactly to separate these.
- NFLX guide cut → channel 1/3 for WBD/DIS/PARA streaming and ad sentiment.
- **LRCX −6.3% AH on a beat-and-raise — the key failure mode.** Fundamentals transferred fine (equipment demand is real); price inverted because positioning was maxed. Any version of this study without a crowding control will read LRCX as "good news is bad for peers" and poison the sample. This case is why §4 mandates the control below.

## 2nd-level analysis — three different things "predict" could mean
The study must report three outcomes separately; they have different consumers and different bars:
- **(a) Peer price reaction** — peer abnormal returns 1d/3d/5d after the leader's print. Interesting, publishable, but the *least* actionable for this book: Jamie doesn't trade direction on peers, and (a) is where the LRCX inversion lives.
- **(b) Peer analyst revision direction** — does IBES revision breadth for the peers move with the leader's surprise sign? **Fildi is already wired for this:** the WRDS research lane syncs IBES, and revision breadth + SUE already feed the production fund score. If (b) holds, a chain-state feature can join inputs fildi already trusts, through the score machinery that already exists — no new plumbing.
- **(c) Peer implied-move / IV re-rating into the peer's own print** — **the money question for a put-selling book.** When a chain leader prints hot, do the still-to-report peers' implied moves and IV re-rate upward (and realized follow)? Two book consequences: (i) selling premium into a peer whose leader just printed a negative surprise is correlated event risk the current per-name earnings window cannot see — each name looks idiosyncratic while the book is quietly making one sector bet several times; (ii) post-print vol-crush windows may cluster by chain (the chain's uncertainty resolves progressively), which is an entry-sequencing fact, not a direction call.

**Prior ordering (Muse's, for Claude to challenge):** (c) > (b) > (a) in expected value to the book. A study that only proves (a) should be scored a partial pass and must not feed any score.

## Study design
- **Sample:** past earnings seasons available in fildi's data (target ≥ 6 seasons), universe = the ~570 names with bars + fundamentals; leaders = names whose print falls in the curated map or a GICS sub-industry with ≥3 covered peers reporting later that season.
- **Leader surprise:** SUE (IBES, already synced) as primary; PEAD-style post-announcement drift of the leader as the secondary/robustness measure. Guidance change (raise/cut vs prior guide) as a separate leader signal where the calendar source carries it — tonight's transfers were guide-driven (SMCI, T, NFLX) more than EPS-driven; an EPS-only surprise variable would miss the motivating cases.
- **Outcomes:** (a) peer abnormal returns (market/sector-adjusted) at +1d/+3d/+5d vs leader surprise sign and size; (b) peer IBES revision breadth Δ over the 10 trading days post-print; (c) peer implied move at its own print conditional on chain state (leader printed hot/cold/flat) vs its unconditional implied move, plus realized/implied ratio at the peer's print.
- **Mandatory control — positioning/crowding:** at minimum leader+peer 20d pre-print run-up and IV rank; short interest is available via the existing LSEG sync. Report results with and without the control, and report the high-crowding subsample (the LRCX cell) separately. **No headline number ships without the crowding split.**
- **Pass/fail bars (pre-registered here; Claude may tighten, not loosen, in review):**
  - (a) PASS: mean peer abnormal return ≥ 25 bps at +3d for top/bottom-quintile leader surprises, t ≥ 2.0 after the crowding control, in ≥ 2 of the last 4 seasons. 
  - (b) PASS: revision-transfer sign hit rate ≥ 58% (vs 50% base) on peers with ≥5 covering analysts.
  - (c) PASS: peer implied move differs by chain state by ≥ 10% relative, and realized/implied at the peer print shifts in the predicted direction (hot chain → realized ≥ implied more often than baseline).
  - Overall: the study "earns a build" only if (c) or (b) passes. (a)-only = archive as knowledge, no feature.
- **Deliverable:** one study note (S-series) with the three outcomes, the crowding split, graph coverage stats, and a per-channel breakdown (demand vs supplier vs multiple) if the data supports it.

## Use cases (if the bars clear)
1. **Book defense — chain-risk flag.** Flag short-premium candidates whose chain leader printed a negative surprise (or guide cut) within the last N trading days and who have not yet reported. Why: the current earnings window is per-name; it cannot see that three "separate" underliers are one sector event. Expected value: fewer correlated assignment/gap events per season; this is loss avoidance on the live book, the highest-value class of feature fildi ships.
2. **Entry timing — crush windows cluster by chain.** If (c) passes, sequence new short-premium entries after the chain's last major print rather than before the first: the chain's IV deflates progressively as leaders report. Expected value: same trades, systematically better entry premium and lower event variance — measurable against the book's own entry history.
3. **Score input (conditional).** A chain-state feature (leader surprise sign × recency) for the fund/put scores — only via (b) or (c) passing, only through the normal score-promotion gates, never straight from this study into production weights.
