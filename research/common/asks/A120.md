## Context
Jamie asked for one plan for Claude to find patterns/indicators across six areas he
specified (intraday reversal archetypes, day-of-week effects, macro/earnings event
patterns, Asia↔US lead-lag, yield/macro recession prediction, historical regime matching)
— plus new research ideas in the same spirit, plus external skills/plugins for pattern
identification, plus the key architectural ask: **stop hand-specifying X and Y**. Build a
discovery engine that continuously varies X and Y (features, transforms, inverses, ratios,
combinations) and tests for patterns on its own, with statistical hygiene. This plan is
that program. It is deliberately detailed: findings here feed trade recommendations, so
the bar is evidence, not vibes.

## Statistical hygiene (non-negotiable, applies to every track)
- **Base rates first**: every conditional probability reported against its unconditional
  base rate. A "70% reversal" means nothing if the base rate is 68%.
- **Multiple-testing correction**: the X/Y engine tests thousands of hypotheses; use FDR
  (Benjamini-Hochberg) or Holm correction. Raw p-values are never reported alone.
- **Out-of-sample discipline**: walk-forward validation; findings must survive on data the
  search never saw. In-sample-only patterns are not findings.
- **Regime conditioning**: Jamie's core observation is that patterns are regime-dependent.
  Every result is reported per-regime (trend/chop, high/low vol, pre/post event) AND
  pooled — pooled-only results are suspect by default.
- **Economic significance > statistical significance**: a 52%-vs-50% edge with transaction
  costs is zero. Report expectancy net of realistic costs for a put seller.
- **Pre-registration** where the hypothesis exists before the test (his six areas);
  clearly label exploratory vs confirmatory results.

- **T5 recession odds:** Use — macro-tab gauge. Expected: Brier < 0.15 vs 0.25 base. Pass: ECE < 0.05 (calibrated).

Kill rule: a track that produces no pass-grade finding after one full run is parked, not re-run.

Phase 1 — Jamie's six research tracks (Claude executes)

**T1. Intraday reversal archetypes (multi-horizon).** Cluster normalized intraday price
paths (k-means with DTW distance, or matrix-profile motifs — see tooling) into archetypes:
open-high-fade-midday-reverse, open-low-rally-fade, full-trend-up, full-trend-down, chop.
Then: P(reversal point | archetype, regime, time-of-day) with probabilistic framing —
Jamie's "clear sign of reversal mid-day" becomes a quantified conditional distribution, not
a narrative. Repeat on weekly/monthly/3m bars for swing horizons. Deliverable: archetype
classifier + reversal-probability curves tradeable as "don't sell premium into archetype X
at time Y" rules.

**T2. Day-of-week effects.** Test Monday–Friday return/vol/reversal distributions with
logical priors (weekend risk premium, institutional flow timing, OpEx Friday effects).
Report which weekday effects survive FDR correction and whether they're regime-stable or
concentrated in specific eras. Deliverable: weekday prior table feeding the morning
brief's "today's tape" context.

**T3. Macro/earnings event patterns.** Pre/post-event drift around CPI, PPI, FOMC, NFP;
post-earnings drift and the recent "earnings met with post-market drops" observation.
Implied-move vs realized-move (ties A101). Jamie's caution is correct: test explicitly
whether these are regime artifacts by interacting event dummies with regime indicators.
Deliverable: event playbook — what usually happens before/after each event type, with
confidence intervals, feeding A79's earnings-risk score and the event calendar.

**T4. Asia↔US lead-lag.** Who leads whom, conditional on *where the news was*: on
FOMC/CPI days the US leads and Asia follows; on quiet days Asia's move may predict the US
open. Use lead-lag correlation with proper lag selection and Granger-style tests, split by
news-origin regime. Include Europe as a third leg. Deliverable: overnight-gap predictor
inputs for the morning brief ("Asia down 2% on no US news → US open implications: ...").

**T5. Recession prediction from yields + macro.** Yield-curve inversions (which
tenor pairs, with what lead time), credit spreads (HY OAS — already in the briefs),
Sahm rule, LEI components. Output a **probabilistic recession odds** (not a binary call),
updated monthly, with historical calibration (Brier score it — ties A85's scoring
machinery). Deliverable: recession-probability gauge for the macro tab.

**T6. Historical regime matching.** Formalize the "1999 vs roaring 80s" debate: define a
regime feature vector (valuation, curve shape, credit spreads, vol, breadth, policy
stance), compute distance from the current regime to historical regimes, and report what
followed each nearest analog (returns distribution, drawdowns, duration). Honest about
uncertainty: analogs are priors, not predictions. Deliverable: regime-analog panel for the
macro tab ("current regime nearest to X; what followed X: ...").

## Phase 2 — New ideas in the same spirit (Muse-originated, Claude validates)
1. **Overnight vs intraday decomposition**: where do index gains actually accrue?
   (The classic finding: most gains come overnight.) If true now, it changes when premium
   should be sold vs avoided.
2. **VIX term-structure shape transitions**: contango→backwardation flips as reversal
   precursors; term-structure slope as a regime feature for T1.
3. **Breadth thrusts and divergences**: advance/decline thrusts, new-high/new-low
   divergences as reversal signals — quantified, not technician lore.
4. **Pre-event drift anomalies**: FOMC drift (documented pre-announcement drift),
   OpEx-week pinning/decay, turn-of-month and holiday effects — retest on recent data.
5. **Stock/bond correlation regime flips**: the correlation sign change is itself a
   regime marker; test its predictive content for equity reversals.
6. **VVIX and HY spreads as leading indicators**: does vol-of-vol or credit stress lead
   equity reversals by days? (Briefs already track both — make them predictive.)
7. **Cross-asset overnight predictors**: dollar, gold, oil, copper moves → next-day US
   equity open, conditional on T4's news-origin split.
8. **Streak reversal base rates**: P(reversal | n consecutive up/down days), with the
   base rate front and center — kills or confirms gambler's-fallacy trading.
9. **Intraday volume-profile / VWAP-deviation zones**: do extreme VWAP deviations
   mean-revert intraday at better-than-base rates? (Complements T1's reversal points.)
10. **Positioning proxies as contrarian signals**: AAII sentiment, fund flows, put/call
    ratios — test the "pain trade" hypothesis with the same hygiene as everything else.
11. **Dealer-flow intraday footprint** (ties A101/A109): GEX-based hedging-pressure
    proxies around the archetypes in T1 — does dealer hedging explain the midday
    reversals Jamie sees?

## Phase 3 — The automated X/Y discovery engine (the core build)
This is Jamie's key ask: stop hand-specifying X and Y.
- **Feature library**: price/volume/volatility/breadth/macro/positioning features across
  horizons (intraday → monthly), plus the outputs of T1–T6 as features.
- **Transform operators**: lag, difference, ratio, inverse, z-score, percentile rank,
  rolling min/max/sum, regime-interaction — applied combinatorially, exactly the
  "inverses or ratios or combination of metrics" variations he described.
- **Search + guardrails**: exhaustive-or-sampled search over (X, transform, Y, horizon)
  with FDR correction, walk-forward OOS validation, and a **stability requirement**
  (pattern must hold across ≥2 distinct regimes or it's labeled regime-specific, not
  general).
- **Promotion pipeline**: surviving patterns get a write-up (effect size, base rate,
  regime scope, expectancy net of costs) and enter the **conviction ledger (A94)** as
  evidence; the best ones become scanner "so what" lines (A81), entry-gate inputs (A52),
  or regime-filter inputs (A56/A115). Dead patterns are logged as dead — no silent
  graveyard, no resurrected p-hacking.
- **Cadence**: weekly discovery run (Sunday, alongside the AI radar digest); a human-
  readable digest of newly promoted / demoted patterns. Claude owns the statistics;
  Cursor builds the pipeline.

## Phase 4 — External tooling for pattern identification (evaluate, then adopt)
- **stumpy (matrix profile)** — the single best fit for T1: finds repeating motifs in
  time series without labels. "Show me every past intraday session shaped like today" is
  literally a matrix-profile query. Evaluate first.
- **tsfresh** — automated time-series feature extraction (hundreds of features) for the
  X/Y engine's feature library. Evaluate for compute cost vs value.
- **sktime / aeon** — time-series ML toolkits for the classifier/regression pieces.
- **Agent-skill packaging** (ties A103/A116/A118): the discovery workflow itself should
  become a skill so future agents run it consistently. Vet before installing (standing
  security rule).

## Token/compute guardrail (Jamie's constraint)
Discovery runs are the most token-hungry thing in this queue if done naively. Rules:
every track states its compute budget up front (data size × method cost); the X/Y engine
runs on a fixed weekly budget with early-stopping on dead branches; LLM reasoning is
reserved for interpretation of statistically-surviving patterns, never for the search
itself (the search is code, not tokens). Report tokens/run alongside results.
