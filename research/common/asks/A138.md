## Context
From @jesserurka's reel ("$24K terminal vs free model on GitHub"). Identified as
**google-research/timesfm** (TimesFM 3.0, Aug 2026; 34.5K stars; Apache-2.0).
Functionally tested 2026-10-16 by Muse: pip-installable, runs on CPU, loaded the
200M PyTorch checkpoint, produced a sane 32-step forecast with quantile outputs on
a synthetic trending series (correctly continued the trend). Test passed — this is
real, not hype.

## Why it matters
A pretrained time-series foundation model gives fildi a forecasting primitive
without training anything: vol forecasting (feeds A137's E[RV] vs IV spread),
short-horizon price path scenarios with quantiles (feeds A134's regime work and
A120's pattern studies). Quantile outputs mean probabilistic statements, not
point guesses — compatible with the Brier-score discipline in A124/A129.

## Proposal (Cursor pilots; Muse verifies)
1. Pilot offline: run TimesFM on SPY/QQQ daily closes + realized-vol series;
   compare its 10-day forecasts vs the HAR baseline from A137 (same preregistered
   protocol, timestamp-clean per A131).
2. Keep it only if it beats HAR on the metrics that matter (directional accuracy
   of vol moves, quantile calibration) — not on vibes.
3. If it wins: wire as an optional forecaster behind the A137 vol-edge signal;
   never as a standalone trading trigger. No auto-trade, ever.

- [quant] review of the evaluation protocol before the pilot counts.
- **Claude plan 10-16:** **Keep as an arm of S033, not its own build.** Same target (10-day RV), same names, same split → TimesFM forecast vs HAR vs RV20 in one table. Cost: CPU only, free (200M checkpoint), ~273 names × daily refits is heavy → test on weekly forecast dates (≈ 15k forecasts). Adopt only if it beats HAR's IC by the same ≥ 0.02 rule; otherwise HAR stays (cheaper, explainable). Never a standalone trigger (agreed).
- **Muse verification 2026-10-17 (inference config for the pilot):** benchmarked TimesFM-2.5 200M PyTorch CPU on a 2-vCPU Linux box (median of 2 batches x 24 synthetic SPY-like series). Horizon is nearly free (h=5 to h=21 costs ~19% at ctx=256 — one-shot decode, not autoregressive); context length is the cost driver (ctx 512 approx 1.7x slower than ctx 256); force_flip_invariance=False gives ~2x speedup (e.g. 258.7 -> 105.9 ms/series at ctx=256 h=10) at 0.17% mean forecast drift (p99 0.57%). Projected nightly batch at ctx=256, flip OFF: 500 tickers ~53 s (Mac M-series should beat this). Recommended pilot config: max_context <= 256, max_horizon free, force_flip_invariance=False. No GPU/server move needed for the nightly A137 batch. Benchmark script + results: weekend-deep-dive/timesfm_bench.py and timesfm_results_2026-10-17.txt.
