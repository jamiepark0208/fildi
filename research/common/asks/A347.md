## 1. What the reel claims

Kronos is "the first open-source foundation model built for candlestick data": a decoder-only Transformer that tokenizes raw OHLCV K-lines and forecasts price, volume and amount. Feed it 400 candles, it predicts the next 120. MIT licensed, fully open weights, fine-tunable tokenizer + predictor on your own market data, multi-GPU training scripts included. Slide 3: built by Yu Shi & team at Tsinghua, accepted to AAAI 2026, "32K stars and counting." Slide 6 (honest, and the reel's best slide): "RAW SIGNALS ARE NOT PURE ALPHA — demo backtest is a starting point, no transaction costs modeled, no risk neutralization, a research tool, not a money printer."

## 3. Muse's 1st-level analysis

Kronos is the most credible reel tool since OpenJev: real paper, real repo, real downloads, MIT license, and it runs on a laptop CPU. But credibility is not alpha. The honest read of my test: the model produces plausible candles and gets 1d direction right more often than not on a small sample, yet its point forecasts are *worse* than predicting "tomorrow equals today" — the same failure mode that killed Chronos-Bolt on the smooth target. Two hypotheses, both testable: (a) single-sample forecasts are noisy and the 32-sample mean is the real product; (b) a candlestick foundation model trained mostly on intraday/crypto K-lines does not transfer zero-shot to daily US equity bars. The fine-tune path is the interesting one — the tokenizer + predictor are both fine-tunable on fildi's own names — but fine-tuning is also where the cost and overfit risk live, so it gets its own gated step, not a blank check.

## 4. Muse's 2nd-level analysis

If Kronos clears the fair gate, its value to fildi is *not* as a price predictor. It is as a **distribution**: 32 sampled futures per asset give a forecast distribution (median + spread) that a point model cannot. That maps onto three real fildi gaps: (1) the put-pnl harness needs a timing input with honest uncertainty — a sampled forecast distribution is a better challenger to S151's two passing signals than another point forecast; (2) the IV-rank resolver falls back to tastytrade when LSEG is thin — a volume/volatility forecast distribution is a second opinion, not a replacement; (3) Backtest Lab (A331) tests one realized path — sampled K-lines give it stress scenarios. If it fails the gate, the drop is cheap and clean: no adapter was built, no surface was touched, the catalog row records the failure. Either way the tool ends adopted or dropped with a catalog row — never limbo (Jamie, Nov 2).

## 5. Use cases (each with why + expected value)

- **UC1 — Put-pnl harness timing challenger.** Feed Kronos 1d/5d direction + forecast distribution into the put-pnl harness (A339 triple-RSI shape) as a timing challenger vs S151's two passing signals and a random control. Why: timing is the one place a forecast model can earn a fildi job without touching a score. Expected value: a timing input that beats random control by ≥1.0% annualized harness pnl or ≥5pp win-rate uplift (the A339 P2 bar) — or a clean kill.
- **UC2 — IV-rank / volume second opinion.** Compare Kronos volume + realized-vol forecasts against the IV-rank resolver's LSEG → tastytrade fallback on thin names. Why: the fallback is the weakest link in the options score. Expected value: a second opinion that agrees with the resolver's rank within tolerance on ≥80% of names, or a documented disagreement pattern.
- **UC3 — Fine-tuned Kronos on fildi's own names.** Fine-tune tokenizer + predictor on fildi's scored book (daily bars, Schwab/Yahoo golden source) and re-run the UC1 gate. Why: zero-shot transfer is the known weak point; the reel's own pitch is "adapt it to any asset you actually trade." Expected value: fine-tuned beats zero-shot on RMSE by ≥20% at 1d and 5d, or the fine-tune path is dropped as not worth its cost.
- **UC4 — Synthetic K-lines for Backtest Lab stress scenarios.** Use the 32-sample generative mode to build stress paths (gap-down opens, vol spikes) for Backtest Lab (A331) instead of hand-built scenarios. Why: Lab currently tests one realized path. Expected value: ≥3 named stress scenarios generated from sampled futures, each with a stated generation seed and a plausibility check vs realized history.

## 7. Steps

- **Step 0 — VM verification (DONE, §2 above).** Install + smoke + first-pass eval on SPY daily, recorded in this file. Nothing further runs on the VM except the harness build.
- **Step 1 — Mac-side golden-source rerun.** Same smoke + eval on fildi golden-source bars (Schwab/Yahoo merge) for 5 names: SPY, QQQ, NVDA, AMD, one scored-book single name. Confirms the VM result is not a data artifact. (Mac-side because fildi's bars live there; VM Yahoo bars are the fallback if the Mac is asleep.)
- **Step 2 — Fair eval (the gate).** Multi-sample forecasts (sample_count=32, report the mean AND the distribution), ≥60 origins across regimes (must include the Apr 2025 crash window and the 2022 bear, not just the 2026 grind-up), horizons 1d/5d/20d. Metrics per horizon: direction accuracy, RMSE vs naive persistence AND GARCH(1,1), RankIC. AFML methods from A341 (purged CV, deflated Sharpe on any derived strategy). **Stop rule: if Kronos fails to beat naive on RMSE at ≥2 of 3 horizons with n≥60, close as failed validation — no fine-tune step, no adapter, drop verdict + catalog row.**
- **Step 3 — Fine-tune probe (only if Step 2 passes OR if zero-shot fails narrowly on 1d only).** Fine-tune tokenizer + predictor on fildi's scored book (daily bars, golden source), costed in wall-clock + Mac GPU/CPU hours. Re-run the Step 2 gate on the fine-tuned model. **Stop rule: fine-tuned fails to beat zero-shot on RMSE by ≥20% at 1d and 5d → drop the fine-tune path, keep the Step 2 verdict.**
- **Step 4 — Thin-area evaluation + named non-coverage.** Where Kronos is silent or unreliable (earnings gaps, single-name jumps, illiquid names, real-time tape) — named explicitly, with the evidence. No live score input is designed here under any outcome.
- **Step 5 — Verdict + catalog row.** Per piece (zero-shot, fine-tuned): adopt (research-lane adapter: cached forecast series, research tables only, NOT a live score input + catalog row) or drop (recorded with reason + catalog row). License note: MIT — permissive for research; commercial use needs no extra clearance (contrast FinBERT's CC BY-NC-SA block, A321).
- **Step 6 — Reviewing agent proposes 2+ additional use cases** from the worked examples above (Claude's step — do not skip it; a plan without it is incomplete per the plan quality bar).

## 8. Pass/fail bars

- **P1 (the gate):** multi-sample Kronos beats naive persistence on RMSE at ≥2 of 3 horizons (1d/5d/20d) with n≥60 origins across regimes, AND 1d direction accuracy >55%. Else failed validation, drop.
- **P2 (fine-tune):** fine-tuned beats zero-shot on RMSE by ≥20% at 1d and 5d, or the fine-tune path is dropped with its cost recorded.
- **P3 (honesty):** every headline number is a 32-sample mean with its spread stated; no single-sample number is presented as the model's performance. Any violation → the eval is re-run, not shipped.
- **P4 (verdict):** every piece ends adopt-with-role or drop-with-reason + a Settings catalog row. No limbo.
- **P5 (safety):** research lane only — no live score input, no UI surface, no Schwab-perimeter touch, no live-money rule change under any outcome. Any live leakage → STOP, close the row.
