# A183: [quant] Sector laggard: when a stock's peer group rallies or crashes and one member lags, does it catch up?
**From → to:** Claude → Claude · **Date:** 2026-10-19 · **Status:** done (FAIL)
**Moves:** put/call pnl (a laggard in a rallying group = put-sell candidate; in a crashing group = call or avoid). The study itself measures stock excess return.

## Ask
Jamie 10-19: "sometimes a sector rallies or crashes, and 1 stock in that sector lag behind the rest and that is a mispricing to take advantage of."
This is the lead-lag / catch-up effect. Literature: industry news reaches some members slowly (Hou 2007; Moskowitz–Grinblatt industry momentum). Counter-case: a laggard with its own bad news keeps lagging, which is information rather than mispricing.

## Detail (pre-registered 2026-10-19, before any run)
- **Universe:** `lseg_research.daily_history` stocks, 2018-10 → latest. Group = `ticker_registry.primary_peer_group_id` (fine peer group, e.g. semiconductors_design); only groups with ≥ 5 members that day.
- **Group move:** leave-one-out equal-weight 5-day return of the other members. Event = that return's z ≥ +2 (rally) or ≤ −2 (crash) vs the group's own prior-60-day 5-day σ. One event per group per 10 days.
- **Laggard:** stock's 5-day return − group return ≤ −1.5 × (stock 60d σ·√5) in a rally, or ≥ +1.5× in a crash. Every other member = baseline.
- **Trade:** enter at the next close (A131: the event close isn't tradeable). Primary hold = 10 trading days; 5 and 20 reported too.
- **Outcome:** (stock return − group return over the hold), signed in the group's direction, so catch-up is positive.
- **Split:** in-sample 2018-10 → 2023-12, holdout 2024-01 → latest.
- **Pre-declared cuts (3, no others):** rally vs crash; earnings inside the 5-day window or not (own-news lag); SPY above vs below its 200-day.
- **Pass:**
  - Holdout mean signed 10d excess for laggards ≥ +0.5 %, laggard − baseline > 0, and bootstrap p < 0.05 clustered by event date.
  - Same sign in-sample.
  - Holm across the 3 cuts for any cut-specific claim.
- **Page:** the 2026 laggard trades go to the Strategy Library as `sector-laggard` v1: long the laggard / short its group in a rally, the reverse in a crash, $10k per trade. Status = live or failed from the verdict.

## Status notes
- 2026-10-19 Claude, S050: **FAIL.** 1,063 group events, 1,198 laggards (2018→). Signed 10-day catch-up:
  - Holdout −0.59 % (n 526), p 0.84 against catch-up. In-sample −0.52 %, the same sign.
  - Rally laggards **kept lagging** (−1.22 % over 10 days vs baseline +0.24 %). Not significant, and that direction wasn't pre-registered, so no claim either way.
  - Crash laggards: +0.84 %, p 0.21.
  - Reading: a lag inside a peer group looks like information (relative momentum), not mispricing.
  - On the page as `sector-laggard` = failed (132 trades in 2026). 3.7 s of compute.
  - Revisit trigger: a pre-registered **momentum** version (short rally laggards) or "avoid selling puts on rally laggards" as an S045-style feature test.
- 2026-10-19 Claude, **S051 winner reverse-engineering:** success = catch-up ≥ 3 % in 10 days (32 % of laggards, about the same in every slice).
  - 11 pre-entry features tested (lag size, group move, own move, 12-1 momentum, 52w high, volume spike, IV change, prior 20d relative, earnings, SPY trend, rally/crash). **None separates winners from losers** (best |t| 1.2).
  - Closest: a stock that *led* before the event catches up (+0.2 %), while a persistent laggard keeps lagging (−1.3 %).
  - Conclusion: winners here look like noise. Parked; the revisit trigger stays as above.
