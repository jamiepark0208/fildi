# Evening Market Recap — Tuesday, October 27, 2026 (post-close)

**Banner:** S&P 7,722.60 (+0.60%) · Dow 52,377.57 (+0.03%) · Nasdaq 26,477.81 (+1.18%) · 10Y ~5.00% (flat) · Brent $113.88 (−1.7%) · VIX 17.52 (−4.7%)

**What moved today**
- The AI trade, day two: Broadcom's $63B financing deal to supply Anthropic with AI accelerators (with Bank of America, Blackstone, Citi, and Morgan Stanley) kept semis bid — AVGO +4.0%, AMD +5.45%, semis +3.02%, neocloud names NBIS +11.2% and optical names CRDO +10.8%, ALAB +10.5%. Oracle also announced plans to deploy Alphabet's TPUs in its own infrastructure.
- Consumer confidence blew past expectations: 91.7 in October vs ~81 expected and 84.2 in September (revised up from 81.9). Present situation rose 8.6 points to 120.4, expectations rose 6.9 to 72.6. The Conference Board credited easing gasoline prices, while year-ahead inflation expectations stayed elevated at 5.8%.
- Oil gave back more of the war premium: Brent settled $113.88 (−1.7%) and WTI $99.90 (−1.83%) as Saudi, Turkish, and Pakistani officials held talks in Tehran — but no ceasefire or binding deal, and Gulf crude exports are reportedly running near 65% of pre-conflict levels. Still near multi-month highs, not a resolved story.
- Housing stayed soft: new home sales ran at a 676,000 annual rate in September (−0.6% m/m, above the morning brief's 653K consensus but down 5.3% y/y), with 8.4 months of supply. Case-Shiller 20-city rose 0.3% m/m and 2.7% y/y in August — still below inflation, a 15th straight month of real-term declines.
- Richmond Fed stayed flat: composite −2 in October, same as September, vs −1 expected. Shipments improved to 0 from −5, but new orders fell to −8 and local business conditions to −8.
- Rates sat still into the Fed: 10Y ~4.99–5.00%, 30Y 5.316%. A hold at 3.75–4.00% is the base case for tomorrow, with markets pricing only ~7% odds of a hike this week and ~73% odds of a December hike.
- Single names: Boeing −10.69% on a quarterly loss — the main reason the Dow finished flat while the Nasdaq gained 1.18%. Coca-Cola −1.64% despite raising full-year guidance. Tesla +4%, Nvidia +~1%, Meta −1.5% on its up-to-$95B capital raise.
- After the close: Robinhood fell ~5% after hours — Q3 revenue $1.42B (in line), adjusted EPS $0.60 beat the $0.56 consensus, but crypto revenue fell 55% while options revenue rose 31% to $399M. Visa slipped ~2% after hours despite a beat, with non-GAAP operating margin 66.6% a hair under the 66.7% consensus. Seagate fell ~6% after hours despite beating and guiding up; NXP −9% and Enphase −14% after hours on guidance; CoStar −11% after hours despite a beat.

**Breadth and leadership**
- Narrow rally, again: six of 11 S&P sectors fell even as the headline indexes rose. Technology (+1.70%) led; energy fell 2.05% with crude. The equal-weight S&P gained just 0.11% vs ~0.56% for the cap-weighted index, and SPY/QQQ volume ran below recent averages.
- Small caps joined today, unlike Monday: Russell 2000 +1.16% to 2,818.87.
- Vol crushed for a second day: VIX 17.52, down from 18.39 Monday and 19.45 Friday. Gold firmed to $4,073.40 (+0.87%); bitcoin reclaimed $80,000 at ~$80,628 (+~1.1%).
- Overnight: Asia chip-led — Nikkei +1.8% to ~63,316, Kospi +2.9% to 5,626 (after a Q3 GDP beat), ASX +0.5%, while Hang Seng fell 0.7% and Shanghai 0.4%. Europe mostly green — STOXX 600 +0.47% to 643.68, DAX +0.71% to 25,590.77, CAC +0.51% — but FTSE 100 fell 0.75% to 10,686.54 as HSBC dropped 5.4% on a revenue miss.

**What changed vs the morning thesis**
- Morning got right: the tech-led bounce extending into Fed/earnings week; oil easing on diplomacy; the 10Y pinned near 5%; and the framing of today as a warm-up — the real events are tomorrow.
- Morning underestimated the data: it penciled confidence at 81.0 and new home sales at 653K; confidence printed 91.7, a much stronger rebound, and sales printed 676K. The consumer looks less fragile than the morning's setup assumed — which, if anything, gives the Fed less reason to sound dovish tomorrow.
- Morning got breadth partly wrong: the setup read as a broad bounce, but the close was narrow — six sectors down, equal-weight barely positive. This is an AI/semiconductor tape wearing a broad-market costume.
- Morning's earnings rule held again: no naked short puts into tonight's AMC prints was right — Visa, Robinhood, and Seagate all slipped after hours despite beats, and NXP/Enphase showed guidance alone can gap a name double digits.
- What shifted: the story moved from "positioning bounce" to "AI catalyst confirmed, event walls still ahead." Boeing's loss is a reminder the Dow and the Nasdaq are different tapes right now.
