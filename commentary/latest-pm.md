# Evening Market Recap — Wednesday, November 4, 2026 (Post-Close)

**Banner:** S&P 7,688.62 (+1.58%) · Dow 53,356.12 (+3.03%) · Nasdaq 25,736.51 (+1.44%) · 10Y 4.89% (−6 bp) · Brent $94.62 (−8.7%) · VIX 16.61 (−20.3%)

## What moved today

- **The election overhang cleared, and the Dow had its best day since April 2025.** Democrats taking both chambers was read as uncertainty removed, not as a directional policy bet — the S&P posted its best session since early August, the Dow its best since April 9, 2025, and both closed off their best levels.
- **Oil collapsed on Iran-war de-escalation hopes.** Brent settled at $94.62 (−8.7%) and WTI at $88.12 (−8.3%), their lowest since August 31, after President Trump said he had no immediate plans to strike Iran and VP Vance blamed the Iran war for the GOP losses. That is a third straight daily oil loss and more than 14% down in three sessions.
- **Yields fell with oil, and that did the heavy lifting for equities.** The 10Y eased 6 bp to 4.89% and the 30Y 4 bp to 5.28%, the long end down for the fourth time in five sessions. Lower yields plus lower oil is a clean tailwind for the broad market — and the reason the rally broadened instead of staying in tech.
- **Gold and bitcoin rode the same move.** Gold finished up ~3.4%, back above $4,000/oz, silver up ~5.1%, and bitcoin up to ~$80,600. Dollar index off ~0.3%.
- **Data helped, it did not drive.** ADP printed 86K vs ~69K consensus (prior 90K) and ISM Services slipped to 54.5 vs 54.8 consensus (prior 54.9), with prices paid easing to 73.0 from 74.0. The September trade deficit narrowed to −$105.0B vs ~−$107.9B expected. Treasury's $125B quarterly refunding came in as expected, with auction sizes unchanged for coming quarters.
- **After the close, the chip prints split.** Qualcomm beat narrowly on adjusted EPS ($2.17 vs ~$2.16) but guided light on profit, and shares fell ~4.9% after hours after closing up 5.3% at $173.19. Arm beat (52c vs 48c expected; revenue $1.44B vs $1.38B) on cautious guidance and slipped ~2% late. The gaps the morning brief warned about printed — and both went the wrong way for anyone short premium through them.

## Breadth and leadership

- Broadening day, not a narrow rally: S&P gainers outnumbered decliners about 3:1, and the equal-weight S&P rose 1.86%, beating the cap-weighted index. Consumer discretionary (+4.02%) and financials (+3.53%) led; energy (−4.72%) lagged hard on the oil plunge.
- Inside AI, the split mattered more than the headline: hyperscalers rallied on their existing data-center footprint, while semis, memory, neoclouds and engineering/construction names fell. Tesla and Nvidia were the notable Mag 7 decliners. Homebuilders, banks, retail, airlines and solar were among the outperformers.
- Rates and vol: 10Y 4.89% (−6 bp), 30Y 5.28% (−4 bp), 2Y ~4.58%. VIX collapsed ~20% to 16.61 from 20.83 — the priced gap day is over, and premium compressed with it.
- Overnight sessions: Asia mixed — Shanghai +2.16% to 3,686.17, Hang Seng +1.67% to 23,919.50, KOSPI +0.62%, Nikkei −1.42% to 62,188.63 after an intraday plunge to 60,030. Europe rallied across the board — STOXX 600 +1.82% to 657.40, Euro STOXX 50 +1.97%, IBEX +3.00%, banks +4.32%.

## What changed vs the morning thesis

- **Right:** the gap-day call. Futures fell as much as ~2% overnight, then reversed into a broad rally, and the Dow was the firmest leg all day, exactly as the morning's futures read suggested.
- **Right:** yields down, gold up, equities up together was repricing, not risk-off — and the 10:00 AM ISM window did not reverse it, because the print missed and prices eased instead of running hot.
- **Shifted:** the morning's AI risk was framed as broad data-center permitting pressure. The tape split it: hyperscalers up, neoclouds and semis down. Policy beta is now a stock-picking problem inside AI, not a sector-wide call.
- **Shifted:** the morning warned not to add short premium before the 10:00 AM print. That caution cost little — the print was soft — but the real premium event was after the close, with Qualcomm and Arm both gapping against sellers who held through earnings.
- **Unchanged:** Friday's payrolls still set the rate path. Today's ADP beat and soft ISM do not settle it.

## Desk flagged

- **CoreWeave credit is flashing stress the equity tape is not.** Its unsecured 2031 debt fell to a record-low 79.5 cents on the dollar after the election. Bond-market distress in a neocloud name argues against selling short premium there on any equity bounce until that credit signal stabilizes.
- **The oil surplus is widening, not just the price falling.** JPMorgan estimates the global oil surplus widened to 1.4M barrels per day in October as Middle East exports recovered. That gives the crude plunge a supply fundamental behind the headline, so energy weakness has room to run beyond one session's geopolitics.

## Book impact

- **Today's rally plus the VIX collapse is premium compression, not free money.** Short puts carried through the session gained from both direction and vol crush — but new premium written now is priced off a 16.6 VIX, not yesterday's 20.8, so strikes need to be wider or size smaller for the same pay.
- **Do not chase the after-hours chip gaps.** Qualcomm and Arm both moved against the print after the close; let Thursday's open reprice them before writing any new single-name premium there.
- **Energy is a falling knife for short puts until oil finds a floor.** With Brent down more than 14% in three sessions and a widening surplus behind it, energy short premium needs a wider strike and a smaller size than the pre-plunge tape suggested.
- **DTE posture into tomorrow: index-based, wide, and patient.** Bank of England Thursday and payrolls Friday are still ahead; keep new premium diversified and index-level, and let Friday's print pass before adding concentrated single-name exposure.
