# Evening market recap — Friday, October 30, 2026 (post-close edition)

## What moved today
- The morning's tech gap became the day's story, then broadened: Intel collapsed 28.58% to $65.76 — its worst day since the 1970s on a weak Q4 outlook — and dragged the PHLX Semiconductor Index down 7.20%. Amazon recovered most of its pre-market drop to close −1.41% at $240.35; Nvidia fell 4.22% and Qualcomm 11.12%.
- The driver is repricing, not one print: Alphabet's Thursday fall, then Amazon and Intel, have the market questioning AI-capex payback time. The S&P 500's worst day since June, per AP, still closed well off its intraday low of 7,527.67.
- Energy was the offset: the sector rose 1.91% as Brent's December contract — expiring today — settled at $117.64 (+2.4%) and WTI at $104.12 (+2.08%), on the Hormuz tanker attacks and the US–Iran overhang. Brent rose 13.6% in October.
- Gold fell while bonds rallied — the day's warning sign: COMEX gold futures settled $3,898.90 (−3.29%), spot near $3,885, and silver fell ~5.9% to $49.28. Falling yields did not save precious metals; liquidation and margin-covering dominated the safe-haven bid.
- Treasuries caught the risk-off bid: 10Y slipped to 4.94% (−4 bp), 30Y to 5.31% (−1 bp), 2Y to 4.56% (−5 bp). VIX spiked as high as 23.93 intraday before closing at 19.42.
- Bitcoin slid with the risk-off tape to roughly $76,700–77,300 after the reported ~$6.36bn Binance hack, rather than trading as an isolated crypto story.
- Asia fell with tech overnight: Kospi −3.48%, Nikkei −2.12%, Shanghai −1.44%, Taiex −2.49% — while Hang Seng bucked the region at +1.46% and India's Nifty added 0.51%. Europe held up better: STOXX Europe 600 −0.36% to 646.75, DAX −0.85%, CAC 40 flat, FTSE 100 +0.07%.

## Breadth and leadership
- Concentrated, not uniform: the median S&P 500 stock fell only ~0.32% against the index's −1.82%, and 226 S&P names rose vs 276 down. Six of eleven sectors gained — energy, staples, utilities, health care, real estate and materials — while technology bore the loss.
- Small caps gave back their run: Russell 2000 −1.66% to 2,811.84, ending four straight gains. Nasdaq-100 −3.46% to 28,384.85; Nasdaq Composite is now ~7.8% below its record closing high, per Reuters.
- Rates and vol context: the bond bid coexisted with an equity break — a growth-scare configuration, not a rates-driven selloff. High-yield spreads widened to 326 bp (Oct 29, ICE BofA) from 314 bp.
